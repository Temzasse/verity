How it works

One click, three layers of checking.

1

Quick findings, instantly

Click the Verity icon. The article text is extracted and a small model running on your own device flags likely loaded language and unattributed claims within a second. Nothing leaves your browser for this step.

2

Smart analysis, explained

An AI analysis reviews the article and names the specific technique behind each flagged passage, with a short explanation in plain language and a confidence level. Click a finding to jump to it on the page.

3

Fact-check with sources

For factual claims, ask Verity to check them against the web. You get a verdict (supported, contradicted, disputed or couldn’t verify) with links to the sources it used.

What it detects

Techniques, not opinions.

Verity judges how something is written, not which side it takes. Arguing a point is fine; manipulating the reader is what gets flagged.

Loaded language

Emotionally charged wording used where neutral wording was available: “shameless cash grab”, “invasion”.

Propaganda techniques

Name calling, appeals to fear, flag-waving, slogans, bandwagon, casting doubt without evidence.

Logical fallacies

Straw man, whataboutism, false dilemma, causal oversimplification, slippery slope.

Unsupported claims

Numbers, events or causes stated as fact with no source: “experts say”, “studies show”.

Missing context

Technically true but misleading: cherry-picked statistics, missing baselines, one-sided framing.

Misinformation

Only when web sources contradict a claim. Without evidence either way, it stays an unsupported claim.

Honest by design

Built to avoid crying wolf.

Fewer, better flags

The analysis is told to flag only clear instances and return nothing for neutral, well-sourced writing. Every flag must quote the exact words that triggered it; flags that quote text not in the article are discarded automatically.

Reported speech isn’t the article’s spin

Quoting someone who uses loaded language is reporting. Verity only flags rhetoric inside quotes when the article itself adopts or amplifies it.

“Misinformation” needs evidence

A claim is only called misinformation when sources found on the web contradict it, and those sources are shown to you. Everything else is labelled as unsupported or disputed.

Quick vs smart, clearly labelled

On-device Quick findings are fast but less accurate, and are marked as such. Confidence is shown for every finding, and AI analysis can be wrong: treat Verity as a second pair of eyes, not a verdict.

Measured accuracy

Precision, recall and false-positive rate on a labelled test set and on neutral control articles.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

Verity isn’t on the Chrome Web Store yet, so it’s installed as an unpacked extension. It works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers.

Download verity-extension.zip
  1. 1

    Unzip the download

    Extract verity-extension.zip to a folder you’ll keep, for example in Documents.

  2. 2

    Open the extensions page

    Go to chrome://extensions (or edge://extensions) and switch on Developer mode in the top right.

  3. 3

    Load the folder

    Click Load unpacked and choose the folder you unzipped.

  4. 4

    Pin it and scan

    Pin Verity from the puzzle-piece menu, open any news article and click the Verity icon.
